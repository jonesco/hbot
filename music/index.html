BIG IDEA
LEDGER LINES ARE
JUST MORE STAFF

When a note runs off the top or bottom, it gets its own little line. Nothing changes: keep stepping the alphabet, one line-or-space at a time.

Off the top, off the bottom

Above the staff: B sits on top of the top line, then C gets the first ledger line. That C is middle C.

Below the staff: F hangs under the bottom line, then E gets a ledger line. That E is your open E string.

BIG IDEA
IN 4/4,
A QUARTER NOTE = 1 BEAT

Four beats in a bar, count 1 2 3 4. Everything else is measured against that one beat: a half note holds 2, a whole note holds 4, two eighth notes split one.

BIG IDEA
FOUR OPEN STRINGS:
E A D G

Thickest to thinnest. Every fret up is the next half step. Find the string whose open note is closest below your note, then count frets up.

Where the open strings live

E is the ledger line under the staff. A is the first space. D is the middle line. G is the top space.
